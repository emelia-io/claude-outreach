# Before and after, by trade

Read this when the user's category is not one of the four worked in
[outreach-offer](../SKILL.md) section 7, or when they have seen the SEO example and still
cannot see what to change in their own sentence.

Every "after" below is built from the seven levers in section 6 of the skill. The levers
used are named under each one, because the point is the method, not the wording.

**The numbers in these examples are illustrative.** They exist to show the shape of a
specific claim. Yours come from your own deals, with a source and a date, or they do not
go in. Copying a number from this page into a real email is the exact failure the skill
refuses.

## The diagnostic

Find the symptom, apply the lever.

| What the current sentence sounds like | What is missing | Lever to reach for first |
|---|---|---|
| "We work with companies of all sizes" | A buyer who recognises themselves | Narrow segment |
| "We improve your <metric>" | Anything checkable | A result with a number and a clock |
| "Our proven methodology" | The method | A named mechanism |
| "Fast, reliable delivery" | A commitment | A deadline |
| "We are confident you will love it" | Skin in the game | Risk reversal |
| "Contact us for a custom quote" | A shape the buyer can picture | An unusual format |
| "We do what everyone does, but better" | A reason to disagree with the market | A contrarian angle |

## Recruitment firm

> **Before.** "We find the talent that matches your culture. Our consultants know your
> market and take the time to understand your needs."
>
> **After.** "We hire embedded software engineers for French industrial companies outside
> Paris, which is where nobody wants to move. Three qualified candidates in your inbox
> within 15 working days, or we stop the search and you owe nothing. 18% of first year
> salary, half of it payable at the six month mark."

Levers: narrow segment (embedded, industrial, outside Paris), deadline, risk reversal,
unusual format (split payment tied to the probation period).

## Accounting firm

> **Before.** "We handle your accounting so you can focus on your business. A human sized
> firm, close to its clients."
>
> **After.** "We do the books for French restaurants and bars, 20 of them. We take your
> daily takings straight out of your till software, so you never send us a photograph of
> anything, and your monthly figures land on the 8th. 390 a month, everything included,
> VAT returns and payroll for up to 12 people."

Levers: narrow segment (restaurants and bars), named mechanism (a direct feed from the
till, no photographed receipts), deadline (the 8th), unusual format (one all inclusive
price).

## Fractional CFO

> **Before.** "I give founders the financial visibility they need to grow with
> confidence."
>
> **After.** "I do the finance work for the three months before a seed round: the model
> the investors will actually open, the data room, and the answers to the four questions
> that kill a round in due diligence. Two days a month, 4,000 a month, three month
> minimum. Nine rounds since 2023, seven closed."

Levers: narrow segment (pre seed round, a defined window), named mechanism (the four
questions), unusual format (a fixed sprint, not an open ended retainer), proof through
repetition with an honest denominator.

## Design studio

> **Before.** "We create brands that stand out. Strategy, identity, digital."
>
> **After.** "We rebrand B2B software companies that outgrew the logo their founder made
> in Figma. Name kept, everything else redone: identity, the 40 screens of your product,
> and the design tokens handed to your engineers as code. Six weeks, 35,000, and your
> developers are in the room from week one so the handover is not a second project."

Levers: narrow segment (B2B software post founder branding), named mechanism (tokens as
code, engineers in the room), deadline, unusual format (fixed price and fixed scope).

## IT services and managed services

> **Before.** "We take care of your IT so you do not have to. Proactive monitoring,
> responsive support, tailor made solutions."
>
> **After.** "We run IT for French notary and law firms of 10 to 80 people, where the
> constraint is that client files never leave France and never leave your control. On call
> in under 20 minutes during office hours, measured and published every month. 89 per
> user per month. If we miss the 20 minutes twice in a month, that month is free."

Levers: narrow segment (regulated professions), a result with a number (20 minutes,
published), risk reversal, contrarian angle (data residency as the headline, not as a
footnote).

## Corporate training

> **Before.** "We upskill your teams with tailor made programmes built around your
> objectives."
>
> **After.** "One day, in your offices, for a sales team of 6 to 20 that has just been
> handed a CRM they do not use. They leave with their own pipeline cleaned up and their
> next 30 days of calls booked in it. 3,200 for the day. If fewer than 8 of 10 are still
> using it after 60 days, we come back for free."

Levers: narrow segment (a team in a specific situation), named mechanism (they work on
their own pipeline, not on a case study), unusual format (one day, in their office), risk
reversal with a measured condition.

## Translation agency

> **Before.** "Professional translations in over 40 languages, by native speakers, with
> guaranteed quality."
>
> **After.** "We translate French SaaS products into German, and only German. Your strings
> file in, your strings file out, keys untouched, plurals and variables handled, in 48
> hours. 0.14 a word. The same translator stays on your product, so the vocabulary you
> chose in January is still the vocabulary in November."

Levers: narrow segment (one direction, one industry), named mechanism (the strings file
and the persistent translator), deadline, contrarian angle (one language rather than
forty is the claim, not a limitation).

## PR and communications

> **Before.** "We build your visibility and get you talked about in the right places."
>
> **After.** "We get French deeptech startups quoted in trade press their customers
> actually read, not in the startup press their competitors read. Four placements in six
> months, in titles you approve before we pitch. 3,500 a month. We do not do press
> releases and we do not count LinkedIn impressions."

Levers: narrow segment, contrarian angle (trade press over startup press), a result with a
number and a clock, a published boundary.

## B2B SaaS, a second shape

> **Before.** "The modern platform for finance teams. Automate your workflows and gain
> visibility."
>
> **After.** "We chase late invoices for French companies billing 50 to 500 customers a
> month. Every unpaid invoice gets its own reminder sequence, in the tone you choose, and
> stops the moment the payment lands. Our customers see days sales outstanding fall by
> around 12 days in the first quarter. 190 a month, no per user pricing, and we plug into
> Pennylane and Sellsy in an afternoon."

Levers: narrow segment (an invoice volume band), named mechanism (per invoice sequence,
auto stop on payment), a result with a number and a clock, unusual format (flat price, no
seats).

## LinkedIn ghostwriter

> **Before.** "I help founders build a strong personal brand on LinkedIn and grow their
> audience."
>
> **After.** "I write LinkedIn posts for founders of B2B companies who sell to a market of
> fewer than 5,000 buyers. We are not chasing reach, we are chasing the 300 people who can
> sign. Two posts a week, written from a 30 minute call every fortnight, and you approve
> everything. 1,800 a month. I turn down anyone selling to consumers."

Levers: narrow segment (small addressable market), contrarian angle (reach is the wrong
metric here), named mechanism (the fortnightly call as the only input you owe), a
published boundary.

## What the ten have in common

None of them claims to be better. Every one of them claims to be different, in a way a
competitor could publicly refuse, and every one names the group it is for and the group it
is not for. That is what survives the swap test, and it is the whole job.
